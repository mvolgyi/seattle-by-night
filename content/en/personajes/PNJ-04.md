## 🧬 Basics

*Earlier source: doc 26, “Helen Ward” (Touchstones). Where it clashes with docs 00–16, those win; see the last page.*

Helen Ward wasn't born looking for monsters. She found them because nobody else wanted to look.


- **Name:** Helen Ward

- **Age:** 34

- **Profession:** Freelance investigative journalist / regular contributor to digital outlets and investigative podcasts.

- **City:** Seattle

- **Specialty:** Cold cases, disappearances, “inexplicable” incidents, police and corporate corruption.

- **Status:** Human, without knowing exactly what David is (she knows he isn't normal, but she isn't putting the label “vampire” on him yet).

## 🎓 Origins

Helen grew up in a gray Tacoma neighborhood, the daughter of a patrol cop and an ER nurse. The house always smelled of cold coffee, Betadine, and exhaustion.

- From her father she learned to **smell a lie** in a statement.

- From her mother, that **most victims never make the news**.

As a teenager she became obsessed with the crime pages:

unsolved murders, disappearances of girls the police wrote off as “she probably ran off with the boyfriend,” fires with “unknown” causes.

She studied journalism with the naïve idea of “bringing the truth to light.” What she found was:

- Editors who told her:

   “This is too dark, it doesn't sell. Give me some light corruption or a sex scandal instead.”

- Law enforcement that closed doors… or flat-out asked her to “drop it.”

She didn't drop it.

## 📰 The Inconvenient Journalist

Helen specialized in:

- **Strange disappearances in specific neighborhoods**

- **Deaths classified as overdoses** with no prior history

- **“Accidental” fires** in buildings with shady records

- **Cases of extreme violence** with details that didn't fit (blood in strange patterns, bodies nearly drained of blood, witnesses talking about “glowing eyes” or “impossible shadows”).

She started publishing in:

- Independent investigative blogs.

- Small digital outlets with some prestige.

- Later, a cult **true crime podcast**, focused on “what the city would rather forget.”

Her reputation became two-sided:

- To some, a **paranoid who sees conspiracies everywhere**.

- To others, the only one who dares to ask **“what if it wasn't just somebody settling a score?”**.

## 👁️ The First Brush with David

The first time she crossed into David's orbit was over a case that **shouldn't have existed**:

- A series of “animal” attacks near the port, with bodies nearly drained of blood but **no clear sign of a real predator**.

- The police quickly closed the cases as “stray dog attacks” and “bad trips.”

- Helen started pulling the thread:

  - camera footage where the figures looked **blurry or too fast**,

  - witnesses who said “the streetlights went out all at once” right before the attack.

At one of the scenes, she got there **too early**.

David was there.

Not in the middle of the crime, but “cleaning up”:

a tall man, too calm for the hour, in a jacket with no insignia but with a soldier's bearing.

She aimed her phone at him, recording.

He looked at her, and for a second Helen felt like she'd pointed it at a black hole.

Instead of killing her, David did something different:

- He walked over, unhurried, without raising his voice.

- He said her name (he already knew it), mentioned a couple of her articles, one of them pulled from the web.

- He described details of the scene that **nobody** outside the police and the medical examiners could know.

And then he offered her a very simple deal:

> “You want the truth.
>
> I want the city not to burn.

> You keep looking, but when something's too big for you, you call this number first.
>
> Not the police. Not your podcast. Me.”

He left her an old flip phone with a single number saved:

**SAFE – no ID.** *(The saved messages are @ID[DOC-05].)*

That was the beginning.

## 🧷 Current Relationship with David

Helen is:

- **A source of information**:

  - she combs forums, listens to police scanners, reads reports, snoops around hospitals.

  - When something “smells like a monster,” she sends an encrypted message:

     “Another rabid dog in Ballard. Two drained. Info attached.”

- **An early warning for a broken Masquerade**:

  - if she sees strange viral videos, paranoid threads, or testimony that “gets too close,” she warns David.

- **An inconvenient conscience**:

  - sometimes she asks too much,

  - sometimes she wants to save someone David would consider “collateral damage.”

She **doesn't know** exactly what David is.

But she knows that:

- He doesn't age.

- He shows up at scenes where he shouldn't be.

- He moves too fast, sees too much, and sometimes his gaze is **too old** for someone “thirty-something.”

In her head, she shuffles explanations:

- Government black ops.

- Military experiments.

- Something… not human, but she doesn't dare put the word “vampire” to it and believe it.

## ⚖️ Motivations and Wounds

Helen is driven by a mix of:

- **Moral rage**:

  - she hates that victims are invisible,

  - she can't stand whitewashed headlines.

- **Self-destructive curiosity**:

  - deep down, she knows she's messing with things that could kill her.

  - But she can't let go of the bone.

Central wound:

- Years ago she investigated a **multiple disappearance** in a building that ended up burning. *(It's the Larchmont, 2014: @ID[LUG-11]; the friend is Danny Ruiz, @ID[REF-02].)*

  - They killed her article,

  - the paper paid for silence,

  - one of the missing was her **college friend**.

- She's convinced the official version is a lie.

  - What she doesn't know is that the case was a Masquerade hunt, probably one where David **didn't get there in time**.

When David uses her as a Touchstone, part of his guilt is that:

he sees in Helen what his daughter (@ID[REF-01]) could have been: human, brilliant, stubborn, looking right where she shouldn't.

## 🎭 How Helen Behaves at the Table

- **Style**: ironic, sarcastic, smokes too much, sleeps too little. Wears glasses when she's exhausted, but won't admit it.

- Talks fast, but **listens better**: she's trained to catch contradictions.

- She's no combat badass: if fists fly, she protects herself however she can. Her real weapon is:

  - a recorder,

  - a notepad,

  - three contacts in the police,

  - and a memory for things others have tried to bury.

With David:

- Sometimes they argue.

  - Her: “You can't just make people disappear.”

  - Him: “Neither can you, and yet sometimes your work kills them all the same.”

- She senses that he drags his own dead behind him too.

  - That's why she doesn't fully demonize him, even though she's afraid of him.

## 🧩 Story Hooks for the Chronicle

1. **The Case That Brought Them Together Returns** *(in the adventure, @ID[A2-E05] and @ID[LUG-11])*

   - Someone is reopening the file on the fire/massacre that shaped Helen's career.

   - The new clues point to a group of vampires David knows very well.

   - Helen gets in up to her neck; if nobody steers her, neither David nor the coterie, she could get far too close to the truth.

2. **The Helen File** *(it's the Ward File: the premise of @ID[AVE-01])*

   - She has a hard drive with years of notes, names, blurry photos, geographic patterns…

   - To a vampire, that material is a near-perfect map of the city's **hunting grounds** and **Masquerade breaches**.

   - If someone from the Camarilla or the Sabbat gets their hands on it, or takes her out, it becomes a ticking time bomb.

3. **The Night She Beholds the Beast** *(developed as @ID[GAN-02])*

   - A scene where Helen sees David in “Executioner's Verdict” or “Last Bastion” mode.

   - Not all of it, no clear details, but enough:

     - bones that don't break the way they should,

     - inhuman speed,

     - eyes with that light that isn't human.

   - From there:

     - does she pull away from him out of fear?

     - does she double down, because she's finally found something “real”?

     - does she start investigating who she's been feeding for years?

## ❤️ As a Touchstone

To David, Helen is:

- **A reminder of why he does what he does**:

  - every time she sends him a case, it's a chance to **prevent impossible headlines**.

- **A bridge to humanity**:

  - she's willing to take risks for truths that don't benefit her.

  - she reminds him that he, too, chose a code when he could have been just another killer.

- **A permanent risk**:

  - if she dies, it'll probably be from being too close to him or his people.

  - her death would leave David without his only living Touchstone, and David would break (see “When He Breaks” in @ID[PJ-01]).
