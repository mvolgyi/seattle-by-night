## 🧬 Basic Facts

*Earlier source: doc 25, “Tom ‘Bear’ Whitman” (Touchstones). Where it clashes with docs 00–16, those win; see the last page.*

Tom “Bear” Whitman never wanted to be anybody's soldier again after he left the Navy.

Then he met David. And it turned out some contracts aren't signed in ink, but in blood.


- **Name:** Thomas “Bear” Whitman

- **Apparent age:** well into his forties

- **Actual age:** 48

- **Occupation:** Ex-SEAL, private security contractor, now David's right-hand man / ghoul

- **Nickname:** “Bear”—for his size, the way he charges… and because when he gets angry, all you want is for him to look somewhere else.

- **Status:** Human ghoul, strongly bound to David's Vitae

## 🎖️ Before the Blood: The Navy Bear

Tom grew up in a working-class neighborhood in Philadelphia, the son of a truck driver and a waitress. He was the typical big kid, strong and slow to anger… until somebody messed with his people. That's when the “bear” came out.

- He enlisted in the Navy at 18.

- He made it through brutal selection and ended up on a SEAL special operations team.

- Specialty: **heavy support, breacher, and long-gun operator**.

  - First man through the door.

  - Last man out of the blast radius.

In the service he learned the kind of violence that doesn't make it into the movies:

dirty operations, “unofficial” targets, cleanups no report ever acknowledges.

He got out honorably… but **not in one piece**.

He carried with him:

- Mild but constant post-traumatic stress disorder.

- A wrecked knee.

- A list of faces he saw in his dreams.

He reinvented himself as a **private security contractor**, first for security firms, then for “private clients” who paid under the table.

## 🩸 Meeting David

In the late 2000s, Tom was working security at a **loading dock in Seattle**, where things went on that didn't add up:

- containers that “didn't exist” as far as Customs was concerned,

- night movements with no route plan,

- quiet guys who never looked at the cameras.

One night, a “live” shipment got out of control:

- abductees, screaming, shadows moving too fast,

- gunshots that didn't seem to stop a figure inside the container.

Tom did what he always did: he went in first.

He saw:

- a pale man with his jaw knocked loose by a bullet, who **kept coming**;

- eyes shining with an impossible hunger;

- a guy in a tactical jacket, completely calm in the middle of hell, who grabbed the monster by the back of the neck and slammed it into the metal until he'd broken more than bones.

That guy was David.

Tom fired, shouted, tried to enforce protocol.

It didn't work.

The next clear image he remembers is:

- the hot, metallic taste in his mouth,

- a deep voice saying:

   “Either you die here, or you get up knowing too much. Decide.”

It wasn't really a decision.

David offered him his blood. Tom drank.

He woke up hours later with no real wounds, his body feeling like it had just come out of a luxury operating room, and a ferocious energy in his veins. The cameras that night had “malfunctioned.”

So had the reports.

And David, sitting on a folding chair in the hangar, simply told him:

> “As of today, you're part of the cleanup.
>
> What you saw doesn't exist.
>
> What's coming doesn't either.
>
> But you'll get to keep fighting for something other than a paycheck.”

## 🧷 From Soldier to Ghoul

The first few months were a mix of miracle and damnation.

**The good:**

- Knee almost pain-free.

- Absurd muscle recovery.

- Physical endurance that made him feel twenty-something again.

- Sharper senses, faster reflexes, that rush right before going into action.

**The bad:**

- David's **Vitae** became the invisible axis of his life.

- If he went too long without it:

  - tremors,

  - bad sleep,

  - a shitty mood,

  - a feeling of emptiness, as if the world suddenly drained of color.

At first he rationalized it:

> “I've been hooked on adrenaline, on war, on booze back in the day…
>
> Now it's just something else. And at least this is good for something.”

But deep down he knows that, without that blood, he'd fall apart.

## 🤝 Relationship with David

The relationship between David and Tom isn't a simple “master and servant” thing. It's more complicated:

- **Brother in arms**:

  - They share tactical language, silences, that way of entering a room with every angle calculated.

- **Toxic father / loyal son**, dark version:

  - David gives him power, direction, and a cause.

  - But he also keeps him **tied** through the Vitae.

- **Absolute trust in combat**:

  - If David says “cover that door,” Tom covers it even if the world blows up.

  - If Tom says “something's off about this job,” David listens.

Tom doesn't entirely fool himself:

- He knows David is a monster.

- He knows he's becoming one himself, on his human scale.

- But he'd rather be a monster in the service of someone with a code than go back to being a loose weapon in anybody's hands.

## ⚖️ Bear's Personal Code

Tom doesn't have the same code as David, but the two overlap:

- **No kids, ever.**

- **No clear-cut victims.**

- **No killing for sport.**

When David has to cross the gray line, Tom sometimes looks the other way… but he feels it.

Sometimes he argues:

> “That guy was garbage, sure, but he had a family. We could've done something else.”

Sometimes he just keeps quiet… and drinks.

## 💊 Vitae Dependence

His dependence on Vitae shows in everyday details:

- He always carries a black metal canteen in his backpack. Nobody knows what's inside. He does.

- When David is away too long, Tom gets more irritable and risks his neck on dangerous jobs to “kill the craving.”

- He tried to quit once:

  - three weeks without Vitae,

  - sweats, migraines, flashbacks to old operations,

  - whole nights without sleep, staring at the hangar ceiling.

- He ended up on his knees on the floor of the haven, his voice broken:

   “Give it to me or finish me. But don't leave me halfway.”

David gave it to him.

And with it, a few words Tom never forgot:

> “You're not my slave, Bear. But without this, you won't survive what you've already seen.
>
> Decide what kills you: the truth or the withdrawal.”

## 🛠️ Role in the “Cleanup Unit”

Tom is:

- **Logistics and fire support** for the Hound of the Masquerade:

  - drives the vehicles,

  - maintains and modifies the weapons in the hangar's arsenal,

  - plans entry and exit routes.

- **Human shield**:

  - puts himself between David and bullets, explosions, or physical dangers that David could sometimes dodge… but that they'd both rather didn't hit him.

- **“Human” face**:

  - can talk to police, guards, neighbors, and security staff without raising supernatural suspicions.

  - acts as go-between when they need someone with a clean ID and a pulse.

In combat, he's:

- the guy who goes in with the rifle,

- the one who holds the door,

- the one who drags the wounded out of the crossfire.

And, if it comes to it, the one who finishes off whatever David leaves lying on the floor.

## 🧨 Wounds and Demons of His Own

Tom carries several shadows:

- **War guilt**:

  - operations where the target wasn't all that clear,

  - orders he followed even knowing they stank of political bullshit.

- **People he saved who then didn't know what to do with their lives**, and came to a bad end.

- Now he wonders if he isn't repeating the pattern:

  - he saves people from monsters…

  - but he also helps monsters go on existing.

Over the years, he's developed a quiet conviction:

> “If David goes too far over the line, if he turns into one of those monsters with no code…
>
> somebody's going to have to stop him.
>
> And maybe that somebody has to be me.”

He doesn't say it out loud.

But every so often, when he looks at the gun he keeps in his personal safe, he wonders whether one day he'll point it at his own master. *(Under that gun is the note from @ID[DOC-12].)*

- Instinctively protective:

  - of mortals caught in the crossfire,

  - of @ID[PNJ-04|Helen] if she's in front of him,

  - of David, even when he knows David would survive anyway.

**Relationship with Helen (if they meet):**

   “Drop this, go back to writing about crooked mayors, this is going to get you killed.”
