# Emily Rose Smith · 1958–1996

*“She stayed with the ones nobody wanted.”* (her headstone, @ID[LUG-12])

Daughter of @ID[PJ-01|David Smith]. Background character, no actor. Main source: doc 24, “Emily Smith” (Touchstones, earlier source), filled out with docs 00–16.

His daughter is the reason David takes the Masquerade so seriously. And also the reason that, some nights, he would rather not wake up.

## What Docs 00–16 Establish

- **Birth:** February 14, 1958 (@ID[AYU-04]). February 14 is the date on SAFE's messages to Helen (@ID[DOC-05]); Helen doesn't know that.
- **Death:** fall of 1996, in Rainier Valley. She was 38. The exact date, October 21, 1996, is provisional (doc 16).
- **Obituary** (@ID[AYU-04], *The Puget Courier*, 10/24/1996): social worker with the King County Department of Youth Services; bachelor's degree in Social Work from the University of Washington in 1981; fifteen years with at-risk youth in the south end of the city; “the one who never hung up the phone” and “the one who took the cases nobody wanted.” Born in San Diego and raised in Seattle by her mother, Anne Smith, who died in 1989 (*San Diego and Anne Smith: provisional, doc 16*). Her father, “Navy Sergeant David Smith,” died in the line of duty in 1960, when she was two. Next to “She leaves no immediate family,” David wrote by hand: *Yes.*
- **The killer:** Ray Dorsey (@ID[REF-03]), an Anarch who used Rainier Valley teenagers as his herd. A week earlier, @ID[PNJ-11|Rosa Iturbe] had given him shelter at the Everett shipyard without knowing what he was going to do. She never forgave herself.
- **The night:** David arrived minutes too late and destroyed the Anarch with a single shot. The backup who cleaned up the scene while David was still on his knees was @ID[PJ-05|Hal Brekke], two years into unlife. To the city, it was a gang settling scores. To Montgomery (@ID[PNJ-01]), it was the reason he started keeping an eye on his Hound.
- **The survivor:** Marcos Delgado (@ID[MEN-10]), fifteen at the time, the only kid who survived. Before he passed out, he saw a man with a scar on his face shoot Dorsey and kneel beside the social worker. Today he's a youth counselor in the same neighborhood (@ID[GAN-01]).
- **The grave:** @ID[LUG-12], in a cemetery north of the city. Someone leaves fresh flowers every Sunday (@ID[GAN-12]).
- **In the adventure:** Emily's night is one of David's two late arrivals (the other is the Larchmont). It's told only in memories, never in detail (Safety Tools). Vance mentions her in his journal: “He has a daughter across the sea and doesn't speak of her” (@ID[DOC-04]). With Krell, a PC telling him about Emily, with David present, counts as one step (@ID[A3-E03]). The letters from @ID[GAN-10] arrive every February 14.

## Who She Was

- **Name:** Emily Smith
- **Year of birth:** 1958
- **Tie to David:** Biological daughter, his only child.
- **Profession:** Social worker in Seattle, specializing in at-risk youth, addiction, and domestic violence.

David knew her alive for only a few years.

When the girl was 2, he was Embraced in Haiti (1960). He came back to the U.S. as **a dead man with a new face**, and the official version was simple:

> “Your father died in a classified operation. A hero. There's no body.”

Emily grew up with the image of the absent hero, the black-and-white photo in the living room, the folded flag. Meanwhile, David watched her from a distance when he could:

- Watching her leave school from his car.
- Following her school records through contacts.
- Making sure money never ran out entirely, through “anonymous payments” or help that always showed up at just the right moment.

When she grew up:

- She became a social worker because she **hated seeing kids broken by poverty and violence**.
- She set her jaw the same way he did when something struck her as unfair.

To David, Emily was:

- His **primary Touchstone**: living proof that something worth protecting still existed.
- The reminder that *he was once truly human*.

He never revealed himself. He always watched her from afar. He never wanted to contaminate her life with what he was.

## The Night That Broke Everything

The tragedy began the way so many things do in the World of Darkness: with **something small nobody took seriously**.

- Emily worked with teenagers from rough neighborhoods.
- One of them, a kid named Marcos, started talking about a guy who:
  - gave them drugs for free,
  - asked them for “weird favors” now and then,
  - made them drink from a shared bottle, and then the nights went blurry.

Emily, being Emily, didn't let it go.

What she didn't know was that this “guy” was an out-of-control **Anarch Kindred** who used the kids as his **personal herd**, and sometimes as messengers in business that brushed up against the Masquerade.

She did what she knew how to do:

- She took notes,
- talked to other kids,
- cross-checked names,
- started building a file to report him—her way, legal, human.

One of those reports, far too detailed, ended up on the wrong desk. Or rather: on **the wrong screen**, with cameras and microphones the Camarilla was also watching.

### David's Failure

David got an indirect warning:

- “Human social worker asking too many questions about a problem asset. Anarch. Possible exposure. Watch or clean up.”

His initial plan:

- Move fast,
- take the Anarch off the board,
- steer Emily away without having to touch her.

But he underestimated two things:

1. **The Anarch's own paranoia**: he realized someone was tying him to his crimes.
2. How fast human affairs can go bad without any need for fangs.

The key night:

- Emily went to see Marcos at an apartment in a half-abandoned building.
- The Anarch decided to **tie up loose ends** and turn the scene into a “gang shootout.”
- The police would log it as a fight between gangs.
- The Anarch didn't know David was already in the area, on the Sheriff's orders.

David arrived minutes too late.

- When he burst into the apartment:
  - the smell of gunpowder and blood was already in the air,
  - the hallway lights were flickering,
  - the bodies of two kids lay on the floor,
  - Emily was against the wall, trying to put pressure on someone else's wound, her hands covered in blood that wasn't hers.

The Anarch took her hostage, laughing out loud, fangs half out, the Beast halfway there.

David had to choose:

- A clean shot to the Anarch's head,
- or try to close in and negotiate.

He chose the shot.

The Anarch went down.

But first, he pulled the trigger with the hand that held the gun against Emily's neck.

It wasn't a dramatic death. It was fast, dirty, no speech. That's what hurts David most.

By the time he reached her, she was already on the floor.

Emily, dying, looked at him. And in that second, her ears filling with noise and the world narrowing, **she recognized him**:

Not as a “vampire,” not as a “monster.”

She recognized him as **her father**.

What came out, in a broken voice, wasn't epic. It was simple:

> “You did… come… after all…”

And she faded out.

In theory, David had another option: he could have **Embraced** her.

He didn't.

He knew that wouldn't be saving her, but damning her to the same hell as him.

And that's what truly haunts him: he decided it was better to let her die than to drag her into his world.

## Why She Is a Lost Anchor

Since then:

- Emily is no longer alive.
- He can't call her, can't visit her, can't spy on her life and console himself thinking *“at least she's still out there.”*
- All that's left is:
  - the grave,
  - the case file,
  - the memories of watching her as a little girl, playing with toys he paid for anonymously.

As a **Lost Anchor / dead Touchstone**:

- Every time the chronicle touches on:
  - **daughters**,
  - broken families,
  - people who die for getting too close to the truth,

  David goes quiet, walks off, or turns hard, and that night he doesn't help the coterie (see “When He Breaks” in @ID[PJ-01]).
- If a situation reminds him too much of Emily, he responds with disproportionate harshness or, the other way around, with a compassion that breaks his code.

## Nightmares

The nightmares aren't subtle. They're variations on the same hell:

1. **The hallway nightmare**
   - He's in the same apartment building.
   - He hears gunshots, footsteps, young voices.
   - He's always a few seconds late.
   - Sometimes he sees Emily standing, sometimes already on the floor, but the worst is when she turns her back on him and walks out through a door David never reaches.
2. **The nightmare of the Embrace that never was**
   - Emily is dying and he feeds her his blood.
   - She opens her eyes, gets up…
   - but she isn't his daughter: she's an unhinged Brujah who hates him for turning her.
   - She accuses him: “You didn't save me. You stole my death.”
   - He wakes with high Hunger, his hands dug into the mattress.
3. **The replacement nightmare**
   - He sees Helen, or any other young human, in Emily's place.
   - The apartment scene plays out again.
   - Sometimes he misses the shot, sometimes he kills the innocent, sometimes everyone dies.
   - The feeling when he wakes is always the same: *“I'm going to do it again. I'm going to repeat the pattern.”*

The nightmares aren't rolled: they're seen. David shows up late to the meeting, knuckles split from the hangar wall, and explains nothing. Bear knows why, if anyone asks.

If the Storyteller has to roll a Compulsion or frenzy for him that night, the first Resolve or Composure roll takes a **one-time penalty**.

## How She Affects David at the Table

- He feels a mix of **hatred and need** toward his role as enforcer:
  - he knows that life indirectly killed his daughter,
  - but he also believes that if he'd been faster, more brutal, more unbending… maybe he would have saved her.
- With young women who remind him of Emily:
  - he tends to be **overprotective**,
  - or brutally cold, so as not to repeat the attachment.
- When someone suggests “using” mortals as bait, as hostages, as tools:
  - the Storyteller can call for a roll to resist **frenzy**,
  - or have his Brujah anger flare against whoever treats mortals as disposable, even a PC.

Emily, as a Lost Anchor, is no longer available to “raise his Humanity.” What she does is the opposite:

- Remind him that **he can lose everything even when he does “the right thing.”**
- Sharpen the question: “Is the Masquerade really worth it, if the cost is lives like hers?”

## Differences from Docs 00–16

- **Seattle and her childhood.** Doc 24 says David watched her leave school from his car and kept a close watch on her as she grew up (the '60s and '70s). The timeline in doc 17 places him outside the U.S. from 1970 to 2010, folio 14 of @ID[DOC-04] puts him in Marseille in 1963 with “a daughter across the sea,” and doc 16 gives as provisional that he arrived in Seattle in the '80s.
- **The mother.** Doc 24 doesn't name the mother or say where Emily was born; Anne Smith and San Diego come from @ID[AYU-04] and are provisional (doc 16).
- **The father in the obituary.** @ID[AYU-04] calls him “Navy Sergeant”; the earlier sources, a SEAL commando.
- **The Anarch.** Doc 24 doesn't give him a name; docs 00–16 call him Ray Dorsey (@ID[REF-03]) and add Rosa and Hal.
- **The kid.** Doc 24 mentions “two kids” dead on the floor and Marcos; in docs 00–16 Marcos Delgado is the only kid who survived (consistent).
