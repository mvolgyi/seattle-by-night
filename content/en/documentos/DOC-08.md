# DOC-08 · Letter from Sheriff O'Connor to Prince Whitmore, 1978

*[Letterhead: OFFICE OF THE SHERIFF · DOMAIN OF SEATTLE · the emblem scraped off with a razor blade]*

Seattle, April 19, 1978

To His Highness, Prince Marcus Whitmore:

I do not write to ask for clemency. You have already decided, and I have been in this city since before there was a port, so I know a decision made when I see one. I write so that it is on the record.

The business at the strike pier was not what you were told. Yes, there was a fight. Yes, a son of my clan lost his head in front of the longshoremen. I stopped him myself before dawn and cleaned up what needed cleaning. The next day there was nothing anywhere. Three days later there were photographs in a newspaper's newsroom. Photographs I did not see taken, from angles where no one was standing.

Someone commissioned them. Someone with patience and friends at the newspapers, who has spent years saying in Elysium that this city needs “a more modern order.” I do not write his name because you know it, and because written names have a bad habit of ending up in the wrong hands.

I am going south. I will cause you no trouble. But I ask one thing of you, as a last service: do not trust anyone who hands you an easy victory. Easy victories always come due later.

Your servant, until tonight,

**Liam O'Connor**

*Sheriff of Seattle (1931–1978)*

> *[At the foot, in a different ink and a different hand, elegant:]*
>
> *Received. Filed. Let him go in peace. — M. W.*

> *[Below, in pencil, nearly erased:]* *C. H. — watch.*
