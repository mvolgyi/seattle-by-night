# AYU-09 · “Your Folder in the File”

## PJ-01 · David Smith {#AYU-09-PJ-01}

**UNNAMED FOLDER**

Fourteen photos. The first is from 2014: the Larchmont, in flames, and him with his back to the fire, looking at the neighbor's camera. The last is from 2025: Harbor Island, under a streetlight.

Same face. Same scar, from the right cheekbone to the lip. Same coat, or one just like it. Same way of standing: feet apart, weight back, as if the ground were about to move.

Eleven years and not one gray hair.

He shows up in four of my cases. Always after. Never during. He arrives once the worst is over and leaves before the police get there. Once I watched him clean up a scene like someone sweeping a kitchen.

He gave me a phone. He said my name without my giving it to him.

I don't know what he is. I don't know if he's good. I know he's looking out for me, and I don't know why.

*Follow up: find out who died in 1960 with his face.*

## PJ-02 · Celeste Arden {#AYU-09-PJ-02}

**2017 · BELLEVUE PARKING GARAGE**

A fifty-year-old man jumps from the fifth floor of an office parking garage. He'd lost everything in a tender offer that went bad that same afternoon. Suicide. Case closed.

But there's a still from the third-floor security camera, at 11:47 p.m. A young woman, dark hair, expensive coat, crouched beside him before he jumps. She's touching his face. He doesn't look scared. He looks calm.

Four minutes later, he jumps. She's gone.

I looked her up. She works in finance. In one of the photos from a 2019 gala she looks the same as in 2017. Exactly the same. Same haircut.

I don't think she pushed him. I think she was there for something else. I don't know what.

*Follow up: why would someone seek out people who've just lost everything?*

## PJ-03 · Ezra Lund {#AYU-09-PJ-03}

**2018 · PIONEER SQUARE**

A thermal camera at the light rail construction site recorded this on March 8 at 3:12 a.m.: a human figure lifts a manhole cover and climbs down. In the thermal image, everything around it has color. The figure doesn't. It's dark blue, like the water of the Sound. Like something that's been dead a long time.

The figure showed up on the same camera six more times that year. Always in the small hours. Always the same manhole.

And one more thing, which I don't know is related: in the last video, before climbing down, the figure looks toward the camera. And the camera goes dark. Eleven minutes.

Exactly like the ones in my building the night of the break-in.

*Follow up: who turns off this city's cameras?*

## PJ-04 · Nadia Ferro {#AYU-09-PJ-04}

**SEVERAL YEARS · N.**

This is strange to write.

N. has produced my podcast since 2021. She's the best producer I've ever had. She passes me leads and I don't know where she gets them. They always come in at three in the morning.

Going through event photos for something else, I noticed: she isn't in a single group photo. Not one. At the episode 100 party, the whole team is in the picture and she isn't. She was there. I remember she was there. I remember she hugged me.

I asked her once and she said she hates having her picture taken. Could be.

I don't like that I opened this folder. I opened it anyway. It's what I do.

*Follow up: nothing. I don't want a follow-up on her.*

## PJ-05 · Hal Brekke {#AYU-09-PJ-05}

**2023 · THE RUNNING MAN**

A cell phone video, Ballard, July 14, 2023, 1:40 a.m. A car tears out of a parking lot. A big man, blond, beard, fisherman's jacket, takes off running after it.

And catches it.

Not in a long chase. In about a hundred yards. The car's doing close to forty. The man pulls up alongside, opens the driver's door while it's moving, and the video cuts out because whoever's filming drops the phone.

The next day there's no police report, no accident, nothing. The car turned up at the port, parked and clean.

I have another photo of that man in a fishermen's bar from 1998. Clipped from a local paper. The same.

*Follow up: the bars in Ballard. Somebody has to know him.*

## PJ-06 · Ruth Abernathy {#AYU-09-PJ-06}

**2021 · THE IMPOSSIBLE REPORTS**

Fifteen medical examiner's reports from the county office between 2008 and 2021. I got them one by one, with favors and patience. They all have something off: a body with “severe unexplained hypovolemia,” a fire that didn't burn what it should have, an “animal bite” with a pattern no animal leaves.

And they all have the same calm conclusion, the same reasonable explanation. Overdose. Accident. Animal.

And the same signature: R. A., night-shift pathologist.

I don't think she's crazy. I think she's very good at her job. What I don't know is who she works for.

*Follow up: ask her for an interview. No. Not yet.*
