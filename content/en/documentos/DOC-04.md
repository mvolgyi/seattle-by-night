# DOC-04 · Journal of M. Vance, Marseille, 1963, Folio 14 (Typewritten Copy)

*[rubber stamp, violet ink:] COPY 2 OF 3*

**Folio 14 — Marseille, March 1963**

The American learns fast, but he learns with rage, and rage is a poor teacher. Today I broke his left hand three times. All three times he raised his guard again without my asking. He did not complain. He looked at me the way dogs look at you when they have not yet decided whether to bite.

I explained the doctrine for the fourth time. Locate: no one strikes what he has not seen whole. Assess: how much he weighs, how much he fears, how much of the man is left in him. Execute: one blow, the right one, and nothing more. Everything that comes after the first blow is the Beast asking permission.

Not all of those I train reach the end. To those who do not finish, I teach the inverted guard, the one with the weak hand forward. It will defend against anyone except someone who knows the complete doctrine. It is a gift and it is a lock. That way, if one of them goes astray, the one who did finish will always know how to stop him.

The American will finish. I have known it since Haiti. He has a daughter on the other side of the sea and he does not speak of her, and that is what holds him up. You must have something you do not say to endure this.

Tomorrow, the cold water.

—M. V.

> *[In the left margin, in pencil, cramped, slanted handwriting:]*
>
> *This is the one you taught me. You never taught me the other.*
