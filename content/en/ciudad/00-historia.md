# Chapter 3 · The City

Seattle was built twice. The first city burned in 1889, and the second rose on top of it, one story higher, leaving the old streets buried beneath the new ones. The Kindred who rule it never forgot the lesson: what gets buried doesn't disappear, it just changes owners.

This chapter describes Kindred Seattle as of November 2026: how it got here, who's in charge, what's being said in Elysium, and where things happen.

## The Buried City (1889–1931)

The Great Fire of 1889 destroyed Seattle's wooden downtown. When the city rebuilt, the streets were raised as much as a story above the original grade, and the ground floors of the buildings became basements, passages, and forgotten galleries. The Nosferatu claimed them before the mud had dried. Ever since, what happens beneath Pioneer Square has been their business.

The city's first Camarilla was small and harbor-bound: a handful of Kindred who lived off the Alaska trade, the gold rush, and the sailors nobody missed. Prince @ID[PNJ-22|Adelaide Grange], a Ventrue from Boston, ruled from 1897 with an accountant's hand. In 1931 she named an Irish Brujah from the docks, Liam O'Connor (@ID[PNJ-17]), as Sheriff, because nobody knew the port better than he did.

## The Fair and the Glass Prince (1962–1978)

In 1962, Seattle hosted the World's Fair and introduced itself to the country as the city of the future. That same spring, Grange left the city “for health reasons” and was never seen again. Marcus Whitmore (@ID[PNJ-02]), a Toreador who had arrived from San Francisco ten years earlier, took the throne with the support of half the Primogen and the silence of the other half. His first act as Prince was to found the Glass Gallery (@ID[LUG-03]) on the Fair grounds and declare it Elysium.

Whitmore understood something Grange never saw: in a city that looks forward, power isn't imposed, it's displayed. His court became famous for its elegance and its tolerance. As long as nobody broke the Masquerade, the Prince didn't care what his subjects did.

## The Fall of O'Connor (1978)

In the spring of 1978, during a longshoremen's strike, a Brujah lost control in front of the workers. O'Connor contained him and cleaned up the scene before dawn. Three days later, photos turned up in the newsroom of a local paper. Nobody knows who took them. The press didn't run them, but the Primogen saw them, and Charles Halloway (@ID[PNJ-03]), then a young Ventrue with friends in the media, demanded the Sheriff's head “for the good of the Masquerade.”

Whitmore removed O'Connor and named Ethan Montgomery (@ID[PNJ-01]) in his place, a quiet Gangrel who had served the Camarilla on the West Coast since 1903. It was a good choice. Montgomery has spent almost fifty years cleaning up the city without asking for anything. O'Connor left for Tacoma with a letter (@ID[DOC-08]) nobody wanted to read.

## The Marseille Journals (1986)

In 1986, a year after Marcus Vance (@ID[PNJ-06]) disappeared in the Carpathians, a broker from Lyon sold the Brujah's journals at a private auction. Vance had asked for his havens to be burned. Someone didn't do it. The buyer was Halloway, who had three typewritten copies made and kept the original in his vault (@ID[LUG-06]). Nobody in Seattle knows, except O'Connor, who heard about it from an old contact at the port and never told anyone because nobody asked.

## Emily's Night (1996)

In the fall of 1996, an Anarch who used teenagers from Rainier Valley as his herd killed Emily Smith (@ID[REF-01]), a social worker, in a half-abandoned apartment. David Smith (@ID[PJ-01]) arrived minutes too late and destroyed the Anarch with a single shot. The backup who arrived afterward, and who cleaned up the scene while David was still on the floor, was a Gangrel two years into his unlife: Hal Brekke (@ID[PJ-05]). To the city, it was a score settled between gangs. To Montgomery, it was the reason he started keeping an eye on his Hound.

*Note: the 1996 date fits Hal's Embrace in 1994. Emily was 38.*

## The Larchmont Fire (2014)

In 2014, an apartment building in Tacoma, the Larchmont (@ID[LUG-11]), burned in the small hours of a March morning. Seven tenants “could not be located.” One of them was Danny Ruiz (@ID[REF-02]), a college friend of @ID[PNJ-04|Helen Ward]. The official version blamed a short circuit. The version the Sheriff knows is different, and it's in the location's entry.

## Gehenna and the Void (2015–2023)

When the Gehenna War broke out, the Sabbat emptied out toward the east, and the West Coast lost its age-old enemy. The Brujah and the Gangrel broke with the Camarilla on a global scale. In Seattle, the two most visible cases were exceptions: Montgomery stayed out of personal loyalty to Whitmore, and David kept working for the Sheriff as an independent. The Brujah who did leave headed north to Everett, where Rosa Iturbe (@ID[PNJ-11]) organized them into something like an Anarch barony.

What's left of the Sabbat in Tacoma is wreckage: Kindred who didn't leave because they had nowhere to go, or because they had something to hide.

## The City That Watches (2018–2026)

The new enemy arrived without fangs. Intelligence agencies discovered the Kindred and created the Second Inquisition. Its American branch, FIRSTLIGHT, opened a small cell on Puget Sound in 2024, the Puget Cell, behind the front of a cybersecurity consultancy in South Lake Union (@ID[LUG-14]). At the same time, Seattle filled up with cameras. In the spring of 2026, Gale Sentinel (@ID[LUG-07]) installed 412,000 smart doorbells across the metro area for free.

Whitmore's Camarilla hasn't taken a single hit from the Inquisition yet. Halloway thinks that's luck. Montgomery thinks it's his doing. Regent Voight (@ID[PNJ-15]) thinks it's a matter of time, and in November she handed the Prince a sealed envelope he still hasn't opened (@ID[DOC-03]).
