# How to Use This Module

**Core adventure:** *The Ward File* (@ID[AVE-01])

**System:** Vampire: The Masquerade 5th Edition. The Second Inquisition (FIRSTLIGHT) is present in the plot as a latent threat.

**City:** Seattle and Puget Sound, November 2026. Main areas: Elliott Bay and Harbor Island, Pioneer Square and the Seattle Underground, Capitol Hill, Ballard, the Eastside, Tacoma, and Everett.

**Play format:** a coterie of 4–5 players using the new PCs (@ID[PJ-02] through @ID[PJ-06]). David Smith (@ID[PJ-01]) and Tom “Bear” Whitman (@ID[PNJ-05]) are allied NPCs who accompany the coterie.

## Premise

Seattle is the city that knows the most about its residents: every phone, every doorbell camera, and every delivery algorithm leaves a trail. Prince Marcus Whitmore's Camarilla survives thanks to the rain, to indifference, and to Sheriff Montgomery and his Hound, David Smith, erasing everything that shouldn't be there. One night, Helen Ward's hard drive is stolen from an apartment in Ballard: ten years of notes, blurry photos, and geographic patterns. Without anyone meaning it to be, it is the most complete map in existence of the city's hunting grounds and Masquerade breaches. At the same time, destroyed Kindred start turning up on the docks, taken apart with the technique of *Controlled Fury*, the doctrine David learned from a sire everyone has believed dead since 1985. If the Ward File reaches the Second Inquisition, the city burns. If it reaches the Primogen, someone gains a weapon to bring down the Prince. And if the coterie gets it back, they'll have to read what the file says. And decide what to tell David.

## Themes

- **The Masquerade as debt:** every secret that gets protected is paid for by a mortal.
- **Parents and children in the Blood:** Vance and David, David and Tom, David and Emily.
- **Surveillance:** in the tech city, the predator is also the prey of the data.
- **Order versus conscience:** does the enforcer serve the law or his own code?

## Tone

Rain-soaked, tactical *noir* with personal horror. Few words, long silences, and brief violence that has consequences. The threat is cold and procedural: drones, reports, and databases.

## Estimated Length

Core adventure: 6–8 sessions. Chronicle hooks: 12 more, to stretch the campaign to 15–25 sessions.

## Conventions {jugadores}

All cross-references use stable identifiers, which don't change between versions of the book and which become links in Foundry VTT:

- **PJ-xx** player characters · **PNJ-xx** Storyteller characters · **MEN-xx** minor NPCs
- **LUG-xx** locations · **COT-xx** coteries · **LOR-xx** Loresheets · **GAN-xx** hooks
- **DOC-xx** in-world documents · **AYU-xx** handouts
- **A1-E03** scene 3 of Part 1 · **P-xx** clues · **FIN-x** endings

Within each scene, the text is always split into three kinds of block, which become separate elements in Foundry:

| Block | What it is | In Foundry |
|---|---|---|
| 📢 **Read Aloud** | Short text to read or paraphrase to the players | Journal page visible to players |
| 🕯️ **Storyteller Notes** | Context, secrets, tests, options, and consequences | Storyteller's private journal page |
| 🩸 **Stats** | Dice pools, Attributes, Disciplines | Actor |

Tests are always written the same way: **Wits + Awareness (Difficulty 3)**, followed by what a success reveals.
